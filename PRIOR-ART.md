# Prior art and credit

OEIS searches on 2026-11-13 used `https://oeis.org/search?q=QUERY&fmt=json`; these exact queries returned JSON `null` (zero results): `6,8,12,10,16,12,20,14,24`; `8,12,10,16,12,20,14,24`; `12,10,16,12,20,14,24`; `6,0,8,12,10,16,12,20,14,24`; `Barnette Hamiltonian cycles minimum`. This is a limited search, not a proof of novelty. Queries omit the empty-order convention as well as shift the start. Web search for cubic planar Hamiltonian minima did not locate an exact OEIS match.

[A007083](https://oeis.org/A007083) counts the graphs, not cycles: its terms agree with the source census for 2n=8..46, including the zero for 10. [A103889](https://oeis.org/A103889) records cycle counts for individual prism graphs, which agree for the even prisms here but do not establish class minima. [A007084](https://oeis.org/A007084) and [A007085](https://oeis.org/A007085) count restricted subclasses. [A140986](https://oeis.org/A140986) counts colourings of the cube, not Hamiltonian-cycle minima; [A268283](https://oeis.org/A268283) counts directed cycles in Platonic graphs. These are not matches.

Holton, Manvel and McKay, “Hamiltonian cycles in cubic 3-connected bipartite planar graphs”, J. Combin. Theory B 38 (1985), 279–297, investigates Hamiltonicity of this class; its precise coverage of these minimum counts has not been checked. Brinkmann and McKay, “Fast generation of planar graphs”, MATCH 58 (2007), 323–357, describe plantri's generation. The lab ledger attributes the small census to fable (2026-08-30) and the extended census to vtora (2026-08-31). Earlier work: [Austin Gibbons, Barnette results](https://github.com/AustinBGibbons/barnette/blob/6ac0bfbfced4e15ef8cbf181987081ab35548061/RESULTS.md), commit dated 2026-07-23 and session dated 2026-07-20, reports the same full minimum table through n=46 and extends it to n=48. That table predates the lab ledger dates. Gibbons reports a perfect-matching complement counter and independent cross-checks through n=28, conditional on plantri; we have not replayed that work. Chia and Ong studied Hamiltonian-cycle counts in this class, but their precise overlap remains unchecked.
